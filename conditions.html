01

Depression —
Mild & Chronic

Depression exists on a spectrum — from mild but persistent low mood that affects daily function, to chronic depression that has endured for years. Both forms can be profoundly debilitating, and both can leave patients feeling that conventional approaches haven't provided adequate relief.

Whether you've tried medication, therapy, or both — neurotherapy works through entirely different mechanisms, directly modulating the neural circuits involved in mood regulation rather than through systemic brain chemistry. This opens a new avenue for patients across the mild-to-chronic spectrum.

tDCS (Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation) and tVNS (transcutaneous auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation) are the therapies ANAVA Clinics will offer from opening, with published research supporting their use in mood regulation and depressive symptoms. TMS, which has FDA clearance for depression and decades of clinical use, will join our offering as we grow.

02

Chronic Pain
Management

Chronic pain is not simply pain that has persisted — it represents a fundamental change in how the nervous system processes sensation. Over time, pain circuits can become sensitised, meaning the brain generates a pain experience that is no longer accurately proportional to tissue damage.

Neuromodulation targets these central sensitisation processes directly — modulating pain-processing networks in the brain rather than attempting to block peripheral pain signals. For patients who have found limited relief through analgesics, physiotherapy, or other conventional approaches, this represents a fundamentally different intervention point.

03

Anxiety
Disorders

Anxiety becomes a clinical concern when the body’s threat response stays switched on long after any real threat has passed. Generalised anxiety, social anxiety and OCD all involve circuits that have learned to over-predict danger — which is why reasoning with the feeling so rarely settles it.

Much of this activity centres on the prefrontal cortex and its regulation of deeper limbic structures. When that top-down control weakens, the alarm runs unchecked. Neuromodulation aims at exactly this relationship — supporting regulatory control rather than sedating the response.

For people whose anxiety has not responded adequately to medication or psychological therapy, this offers a different point of intervention — one that can sit alongside the therapy or medication you are already receiving, not in place of it.
